Lunar Landing Site Navigator · Sun & Earth from any point on the Moon

1 · Pick scenarios

Each card is one site + date. Choose a mission or crater from the list, or type any coordinates. Duplicate a card and nudge its date to see what a launch slip does at the same site.

2 · Read the sky

The sky strip shows where the Sun and Earth are relative to the horizon (azimuth across, elevation up). Hollow discs are below the horizon. Dark grey is your terrain mask.

3 · Compare windows

The timeline is measured in days after touchdown (T+). Lanes show when each site has power (Sun), a direct link (Earth) and both. The table ranks scenarios; green marks the best value in each row.

4 · Find a date

The landing-window finder scans up to a year of touchdown dates and shows how long sunlight and Earth contact last afterwards, so you can see which dates satisfy your mission length.

Method, accuracy and limits
  • Ephemeris: Moon and Sun positions from Astronomy Engine (MIT). Libration and sub-solar point follow Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms ch. 53 (optical + physical libration) in the Mean-Earth/Polar-axis frame used by LOLA, Quickmap and Moon Trek.
  • Geometry: spherical Moon (R = 1737.4 km); Earth lunar-parallax (up to 0.26°) is included. No atmosphere, so no refraction and no twilight. Times are UTC.
  • Terrain: by default the horizon is the smooth-sphere horizon. Use the terrain mask (uniform angle) or import a horizon profile (CSV: azimuth°, elevation°) derived from LOLA through Quickmap / Moon Trek for real topography. Polar results with no mask are optimistic.
  • Rules: Sun “up” = upper limb (or centre) above the horizon + minimum elevation; Earth “in view” = centre above the horizon + minimum elevation. Event times are refined to seconds; events shorter than ~15 min can be missed.
  • Not modelled: Earth/Sun eclipses of the site, antenna/pointing limits, dust, thermal limits, site tilt, and the Earth-disc size in link budgets.
